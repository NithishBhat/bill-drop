Dear Neighbor, 
Your current electricity supplier is Eversource Basic Service. 
This means Eversource delivers and supplies your electricity. 
As of May 2026, your new supplier will be Boston Community 
Choice Electricity (BCCE). For residential customers, you will 
see a 9% reduction on your BCCE supply rate compared to 
your current Eversource Basic Service rate. Commercial 
customers will see a 5% reduction compared to their current 
supply rate. Both residential and commercial customers have 
the option to lower their rate further by switching to Optional 
Basic at boston.gov/bcce. 
What Is BCCE? 
Boston Community Choice Electricity (BCCE) is a City of 
Boston program providing residents and businesses with 
affordable renewable energy. BCCE stays affordable because 
the City of Boston gets electricity in bulk from an energy 
supplier and passes savings on to account holders. Nearly 
230,000 Boston households and businesses are currently 
enrolled, with residents saving an average of $200 each year. 
Direct Energy, the current supplier for BCCE, provides 
electricity supply for all customers enrolled in the Program

BCCE Optional Plans & Utility Assistance 
Residents receiving public assistance who have a household 
income at or below 60% of the state median income may be 
eligible for discounts on their electricity bill. Participation in 
BCCE will not interfere with any Eversource credits, payment 
plans, discounts, or assistance. For additional savings, you can 
opt-down to Optional Basic (currently $0.13644/kWh). Visit 
boston.gov/energy for more information. 
Opt-up to Green 100 (currently $0.16231/kWh), an easy and 
affordable option to support clean energy. All of the renewable 
energy in the Program is generated in New England. By joining 
Green 100, you will play a critical role in reducing pollution, 
protecting our environment, and helping Boston become 
carbon-neutral. Opt-up today at boston.gov/bcce. 
Commonly Asked Questions 
• What is the difference between Supply and Delivery? 
The supply charge on your electricity bill is the cost of the 
electricity you use during each billing cycle. The delivery 
charge is the cost to maintain the electric distribution and 
transmission lines and manage your account, which 
includes generating your monthly bill, reading your meter, 
and processing payments. 
o You cannot choose who delivers your electricity but 
you can choose who supplies your electricity. 
Changing your supplier will have no effect on your 
delivery charges.

• Will this affect my electricity assistance benefits or 
payment plans? No, you will continue to receive those 
benefits from Eversource. 
• I already chose a competitive supplier on my own, 
what should I do? You must opt-out of BCCE to avoid 
any contract violations. You can opt-out by mailing back 
the enclosed opt-out card, filling out the opt-out form at 
boston.gov/bcce, or calling Direct Energy, the City’s 
current electricity supplier, at (855) 402-5868. 
o Account holders who would prefer to have BCCE as 
their supplier instead of their current competitive 
electric supplier should contact BCCE for further 
assistance or visit boston.gov/protect. 
• I have a tax-exempt account, what do I need to do? To 
retain your tax-exemption status you must send a copy of 
your Energy Exempt Certificate via email to 
usn.taxexemption@directenergy.com, fax to  
(800) 504-7428, or mail to Direct Energy Attn: USN Tax 
Exemption at P.O. Box 180, Tulsa, OK 74101-0180. 
• I have solar panels or community solar, will I continue 
to receive credits? Yes, you will continue to receive net 
metering or on-bill credits and the value of these credits 
will not be altered by participating in BCCE. 
• I am concerned about my overall energy usage and 
costs, where can I get help? Visit the Boston Energy 
Saver website at boston.gov/save.
